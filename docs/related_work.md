| 2.2 | [X Hu, PY Chen, TY Ho. **"RADAR: Robust AI-Text Detection via Adversarial Learning"** (2023)](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-0662) | They propose **RADAR**, a framework that **trains an AI-text detector and a paraphraser in an adversarial two-player game**: the paraphraser learns to rewrite AI-generated text so it evades the detector, while the detector learns to recognize both original and paraphrased AI text. | 2.3 | [Yuxia Wang et al. **"M4GT-Bench: Evaluation Benchmark for Black-Box Machine-Generated Text Detection"** (2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.218/) | They construct an evaluation benchmark for black-box machine-generated text detection called **M4GT-Bench**, built on a M4 corpus. It defines **three tasks**: (1) mono-lingual and multi-lingual **binary detection**; (2) **multi-way detection** --- identifying which particular generator produced the text; and (3) **change-point detection** --- precisely estimating the word boundary between human-written and machine-generated content in mixed texts. | **high** (Task 3 provides mixed human–machine texts with a known human/machine boundary --- token-level ground truth against which we can compare detector attributions; optional extension in the simplified plan)<br><br>Use in *Introduction*: their same-domain/same-generator training requirement is further evidence that detectors learn dataset-specific cues rather than stable signals. | **high** (peer-reviewed on conference and many citations) | [**Yes**](https://github.com/mbzuai-nlp/M4GT-Bench) | **Task 1:** Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1<br>**Task 2:** per-label F1, Precision, Recall + F1-macro and accuracy (7 labels)<br>**Task 3:** boundary localization |
| 3 | [Loris Schoenegger, Yuxi Xia, Benjamin Roth. **"An Evaluation of Explanation Methods for Black-Box Detectors of Machine-Generated Text"** (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14252) | The first systematic evaluation of explanation quality for machine-generated-text (MGT) detectors. It pairs SHAP, LIME and Anchor with three detectors (fine-tuned RoBERTa from Guo et al., OpenAI/Solaiman RoBERTa out-of-distribution, and zero-shot DetectGPT) on a 305-document subset of the H3 dataset. Faithfulness is tested with a pointing game on synthetic hybrid documents and a token-removal experiment; stability with consistency, continuity and contrastivity; usefulness with a forward-simulation user study (36 participants). SHAP is best on all automated metrics (pointing game 0.692 vs. 0.565 random). LIME does not beat random (0.546), and users' accuracy at predicting the detector drops by 13.1% (p = 0.006) after seeing LIME explanations. | **High** (the closest existing work to the project: same object, same methods (SHAP/LIME), ready-made evaluation protocol. The authors note that token-removal tests assume the detector behaves predictably on partial input, which is questionable because omitting words may itself be a signal for MGT. Their faithfulness tests are proxies, not true interventional/causal validation, which is the gap the project can fill) | **Medium-high** (systematic design, significance tests, released code/data. Limitations: preprint, one English dataset of short texts (50–150 words), only 305 documents, 36 participants, black-box methods only, DetectGPT with 5 instead of 100 perturbations, explanation-length confound, mask-token replacement as perturbation strategy) | **Yes** (Zenodo: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15655989) | **Metrics:** pointing game on hybrid documents, token-removal accuracy curves, consistency / continuity (Krippendorff's α), contrastivity, forward simulation accuracy change (McNemar), perceived usefulness.<br><br>**Resources:** H3 dataset, detectors (Guo RoBERTa acc 0.99, Solaiman acc 0.92, DetectGPT-pythia-70m acc 0.74), SHAP Partition Explainer, LIME, Anchor. |
| 3 | [Julian Tritscher, Anna Krause, Andreas Hotho. **"Feature relevance XAI in anomaly detection: Reviewing approaches and challenges"** (2023)](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2023.1099521) | A peer-reviewed review of local post-hoc feature-relevance XAI for anomaly detectors: data-specific, perturbation-based (LIME, SHAP), gradient-based (Saliency, gradient×input, Integrated Gradients, LRP) and model-specific. Showcases on an image dataset (MVTec) and a tabular dataset (ERP) show that the choice of reference values for "removing" features strongly affects explanation quality and can produce adversarial, off-manifold inputs, linking this to counterfactual explanations. | **Medium** (not about text, but methodologically close: it treats explanations of *detectors* and shows that perturbation/reference choices can introduce new signal or off-manifold inputs (directly relevant to token masking). Limitations: only two datasets, one model each, no significance tests, evaluates plausibility rather than causal faithfulness) | **Yes (stated).** https://professor-x.de/feature-relevance-AD (link not verified) | **Metrics:** ROC-AUC and cosine similarity against ground-truth explanations, random baselines (noise, noise×input).<br><br>**Datasets:** MVTec AD (images), ERP (tabular); neither usable for text detection. |
| 4 | [Eldar David Abraham, Karel D'Oosterlinck, Amir Feder, Yair Ori Gat, Atticus Geiger, Christopher Potts, Roi Reichart, Zhengxuan Wu. **"CEBaB: Estimating the Causal Effects of Real-World Concepts on NLP Model Behavior"** (NeurIPS 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14140) | Casts model explanation as a causal inference problem: estimating the causal effect of real-world concepts on a model's output. 2,299 OpenTable reviews were edited by crowdworkers to change one aspect (food, service, ambiance, noise), yielding 15,089 texts with aspect-level and review-level labels. The core metric, ICaCE-Error, compares the *actual* change in a classifier's output between an original and its counterfactual with the effect *predicted* by an explanation method. Seven concept-based explainers are tested on BERT, RoBERTa, GPT-2 and an LSTM; most fail to beat a simple "approximate counterfactual" baseline. | **High as a protocol** (the three-step logic is exactly what the project needs: (1) intervene on the input to change one concept while holding the rest fixed, (2) observe the effect on the model, (3) score explanations by how well they predict that effect. Transfer challenges: one task (restaurant sentiment), concept-level explainers (not token-level SHAP/LIME), no human-written counterfactual pairs for detectors, interventions can create implausible inputs) | **High** (NeurIPS 2022; careful design: grouped splits, crowdworker validation, 5 seeds, four models. Limitations: single task and domain, edit noise, no significance tests) | **Partly** (dataset on Hugging Face (`cebab`); explainer implementations not verified) | **Metrics:** ICaCE, ICaCE-Error (cosine / L2 / normdiff), CaCE, empirical ATE.<br><br>**Resources:** CEBaB dataset, fine-tuned BERT / RoBERTa / GPT-2 / LSTM. Restaurant reviews only, not usable directly for MGT detection. |

| 5 | [Christopher J. Anders, Plamen Pasliev, Ann-Kathrin Dombrowski, Klaus-Robert Müller, Pan Kessel. **"Fairwashing Explanations with Off-Manifold Detergent"** (ICML 2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/anders20a.html) | Shows theoretically and experimentally that for any classifier one can construct another one that behaves the same on the data (same train/val/test error) but has arbitrarily manipulated explanation maps. | 5 | [Juyeon Heo, Sunghwan Joo, Taesup Moon. **"Fooling Neural Network Interpretations via Adversarial Model Manipulation"** (NeurIPS 2019)](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2019/poster/13476) | Defines *adversarial model manipulation*: a fine-tuning step that radically alters explanations without hurting the accuracy of the original model, by putting the interpretation result directly into the penalty term of the loss. Two types of fooling (passive and active); the fooling generalizes to the whole validation set and transfers to other interpreters. Tested on VGG19, ResNet50, DenseNet121 with LRP, Grad-CAM, SimpleGrad. | **high** (this is essentially the recipe for our washing detector: fine-tune a copy of RoBERTa with an explanation penalty while keeping predictions. | 5 | [Junlin Wang, Jens Tuyls, Eric Wallace, Sameer Singh. **"Gradient-based Analysis of NLP Models is Manipulable"** (2020)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.05419v1.pdf) | Shows that gradient-based token attributions of NLP models can be manipulated. | 5 | [Adam Ivankay, Ivan Girardi, Chiara Marchiori, Pascal Frossard. **"Fooling Explanations in Text Classifiers"** (ICLR 2022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.03178) | Introduces TextExplanationFooler (TEF), an attack that changes the text imperceptibly so that explanations change considerably while the classifier's prediction stays the same.
